TSP heuristic lab · manual beam console

Aim the beams yourself. Move the cursor when you decide.

No algorithm runs on this page. There is a cursor, three far corners, and a beam request you can fire at any node. Ask a node for its two nearest, or its two farthest (the beam width is adjustable), and the rays stay on the map until you clear them — so you can put several answers side by side, argue with them, and only then move the cursor to the city you pick. Every move is one real edge; the tour is whatever path you walked.

How to use the console

The map is the only input. The cursor starts on the first endpoint of the farthest pair — that is corner Z's partner, and the pole A–Z stays drawn across the map. Click any city to make it the focus; the beam buttons always ask the focused node, so "ask any node" is just click-then-ask. Requests pile up and stay drawn across moves until you clear them or ask again — that is why the rays are dashed: they are answers, not edges. Every beam button is a toggle, so a second click also takes its answer back.

1 · ask

nearest ×k returns the k closest cities to the focus and draws a plain ray to each. It searches the whole map, walked cities included, but leaves out the ones already wired to the focus — after A → B the nearest from B no longer names A, and the same goes for the city a jump left behind — and any city that already carries two walked edges, since nothing more can be wired into it. farthest ×k does not stop at its target: a faint ray names the k farthest, then a bright leg leaves each of them for that city's farthest city — a bounce, so you see where the far end of the map reflects back to. Excluding the source keeps it a reflection rather than a retrace. By default requests skip cities already on the path; tick all cities to let them name walked ones too. Clicking the same button again removes its answer. The nearest chips carry a ↯ n badge: the number of far and corner rays that land on that city right now, so a city pulled from two directions shows up before you move.

2 · the three corners

The purple dashed line is the pole: the farthest pair in the map, labelled with its length. Z is its far end. The other two corners are the endpoints of the pair that maximises distance × |sin of the angle against the farthest pole — the longest pair that is roughly perpendicular to it. ask 3 corners makes all three report their k farthest cities at once; click it again to remove all three answers.

3 · decide, then move

Shift-click a city, click a result chip, or focus it and press move cursor → focus. That claims the edge; the beams stay where they are, so you can keep reading an old answer after moving. A move onto an unvisited city extends the leg. A move onto a city that already has one edge joins two loose ends instead: that is how a walk split by jumps gets stitched back together, and how you close the loop by hand when the cursor's loose end meets another one. Two full ends never connect, and if the final link leaves every city with two edges the tour closes itself.

3b · jump instead

Double-click a city, or press jump cursor → focus (key j), to move the cursor without drawing an edge — to a fresh city or back to one you already walked, so you can re-ask beams anywhere. The leg walked so far is sealed, a new leg starts at the jump target, and the beams stay put. A jump to a walked city branches the walk there, which Close tour then refuses until Undo takes the jumps back.

4 · close

Close tour connects the last pair of loose ends once every city is in, writing the blue closing edge. With more than two loose ends it refuses and names them, so you can link them yourself first. The gap is measured against Held–Karp (n ≤ 20) or a proven 1-tree bound.

Nothing here is automatic. The beams are queries, the cursor moves only when you say so, and the path is exactly the edges you accepted. That makes the console a place to test a rule before any rule is written.

Fine print. This page is a manual explorer, not the heuristic — src/farthest/farthest_pole.js still holds the Farthest-Beam Walk that decides by itself, but the console never calls it. A beam request is O(n) over the distance matrix and never mutates the path. Each button is a toggle: the second click removes exactly the answer the first click added. The pole line is the rank-1 pair, so it is also the pair the ranked list marks start. The cursor can only move to a city that is not already on the path, so the walked path is always simple and closing it yields a valid tour; Undo and Reset walk rebuild that state, and a jump seals the current leg instead of inventing an edge, so nothing is ever drawn that you did not accept or explicitly refuse. Distances are Euclidean on the unit square, so the numbers are map units, not kilometres. The demo caps n at 60 to keep the requests and the ranked list readable. For the full polynomial solver in this repo (BGMA) — claim: the closest ever to P = NP, but not! — it gives optimum solutions in n up to 400 and deviates from the optimum by up to ~1% when n grows to 1000.
