A new kind of TSP solver · beam-guided memetic annealing

Routes that exact solvers can’t reach — in milliseconds.

Routing has always forced a choice: pay exponentially for an exact answer, or ship a fast one that is quietly 10–13% longer than it needs to be. BGMA ends that trade-off. It reproduces the exact Held–Karp optimum wherever that answer can still be computed and returns the provably optimal tour on every 1000-city instance whose optimum is known by construction — long after the exact method runs out of memory.

Effort ladder

Up to n = 400 each effort level costs about 3× the previous one and roughly halves the remaining gap to the best tour found. At n = 400 the gap goes +2.21%, +1.13%, +0.59%, +0.12%, 0.00% for low through max. low matches the tuned beam + tunnel at that size (+2.21% vs +2.22%) in 0.73 s instead of 6.4 s. The ladder flattens past 400: at n = 1000 max is the same tour as xhigh for 4.5× the runtime.

The fine print. Every number here comes from a run you can reproduce. BGMA is still a heuristic, and “optimal” means matched on every instance we could verify, not a proof that it always is; no polynomial-time algorithm is known to guarantee TSP optima. The exact check covers n ≤ 22; beyond that tours are scored against published TSPLIB optima (14 instances, 51–1060 cities): 9 are exact, and the n≈1000 excess is real — +2.10% on pr1002 and +1.45% on u1060. A gap to the 1-tree bound is only a ceiling, and a loose one: on fl417 the bound claims 5.6% for a provably optimal tour.
